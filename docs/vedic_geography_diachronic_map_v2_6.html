Vectors (1–4 are hypotheses; only 5 is stated in a text)
Premises behind the east → west default (H1)
  1. P1 Viśvāmitra is Sudās’s earlier priest, Vasiṣṭha the later (RV 3.53.9, 7.18.21; Witzel and Talageri agree; the EJVS Ravi study reverses it).
  2. P2 The Vipāś–Śutudrī crossing (RV 3.33) belongs to that Viśvāmitra phase. Sudās is not named in 3.33; the link is via 3.53.9 and the shared cattle (gavya) motive.
  3. P3 The Paruṣṇī battle belongs to the Vasiṣṭha phase (RV 7.18.21). No verse pairs Viśvāmitra with the Paruṣṇī, Yamunā or Dāśarājña.
  4. Geography Beas–Sutlej lies east of the Ravi. P1–P3 + geography ⇒ westward. If P1 fails, the flip (H2) applies.
Premises behind the Pūru west → east line
  1. P1 Pūrus stand at the Paruṣṇī (RV 7.18.13) and dwell on the Sarasvatī (RV 7.96.2). Both verses are Layer 1, so the layers do not say which came first.
  2. P2 The Pūru line continues into the Kurus: Kuruśravaṇa is called Trāsadasyava, “descendant of Trasadasyu” (RV 10.33.4, Layer 2). Whether kuruśravaṇa is a king’s name is debated.
  3. P3 Kurukṣetra is the Kuru centre in Layer 4 (site 13; Brāhmaṇa cites unchecked).
  4. P4 Dotted leg: the Suvāstu (Swat) is a soma-place in a hymn that names Trasadasyu (RV 8.19.36–37, Layer 2). It marks a rite, not a residence, and it is a later layer than the Paruṣṇī and Sarasvatī pins.
  5. What the pins show The Pūru-linked pins shift east from L1 to L4 only if P2 holds. The far-west pin (P4) is not the earliest. No verse narrates a Pūru move; west → east is also the usual scholarly model.
Ikṣvāku / Aikṣvāka in the Vedic texts (listed, not plotted)
  • Ikṣvāku · RV 10.60.4 (L2) · prospers “under the rule” (upa vrate) of King Asamāti, lord (satpati) of Bhajeratha. A person, not a dynasty. Asamāti tramples the Paṇis (10.60.6). Bhajeratha ≈ the later Ikṣvāku king Bhagīratha is a name-match hypothesis (same root √bhaj).
  • “The ancient Ikṣvāku” · AVŚ 19.39.9 (L3) · knew the kuṣṭha plant (see site 31). Now a figure of the past.
  • Purukutsa, aikṣvāko rājā · ŚB 13.5.4.5 (L4) · performed an aśvamedha. In the Rigveda he is a Pūru king (Trasadasyu’s father). Here Daurgaha is the sacrificial horse, not his father; “son of Durgaha” is Sāyaṇa’s reading.
  • Hariścandra Vaidhasa Aikṣvāka · AB 7.13.1 (L4) · “a king without a son” (rājā aputraḥ, not “a king’s son” as the RAG split it); the Śunaḥśepa story. No place named.
  • Tryaruṇa Traidhātva Aikṣvāka · PB 13.3.12 (L4) · his chariot kills a Brahmin boy; his priest Vṛśa Vaijāna revives him with a sāman. RV 5.27 has a Tryaruṇa Traivṛṣṇa beside Trasadasyu: same king, different patronymic?
  • Bhagīratha Aikṣvāka · JUB 4.6.1 · not in your index; details unverified (read Oertel’s edition).
  • No Vedic passage checked so far names a place for an Aikṣvāka or calls a Kosala king Aikṣvāka. Ikṣvāku = Kosala/Ayodhyā is epic and Purāṇic. The stages: a name (RV) → an ancestor (AV) → a clan label applied to kings from other lines (Brāhmaṇas) → the solar dynasty (Purāṇas).

Early vs. Later Vedic Geography

Comparing the early Rigvedic family books (Mandalas 2–7) with the later layers (Late Rigveda, Atharvaveda, Yajurveda Saṃhitās, Brāhmaṇas). The early layer already attests a river chain in the Sudās cycle — Paruṣṇī (RV 7.18.8), Vipāś and Śutudrī (RV 3.33), Yamunā (RV 7.18.19) — but no cited verse states the direction of travel, so the map treats every campaign or migration arrow as a hypothesis.

Dimension Early Vedic (Layer 1: RV 2–7) Later Vedic (Layers 2–5)
Where the groups are placed Bharatas on the Sarasvatī–Dṛṣadvatī–Āpayā [RV 3.23.2–4]; Pūrus on the Sarasvatī [RV 7.96.2]; Sudās at the Paruṣṇī [RV 7.18.8]. Madhyadeśa (Kurukṣetra, Doab) as the Kuru-Pañcāla centre — toponym verses still to be pinned (see site 13–14 notes).
Key Rivers Attested Paruṣṇī [RV 7.18.8], Vipāś & Śutudrī [RV 3.33.1], Yamunā [RV 7.18.19], Sindhu [RV 4.30.12], Sarasvatī/Dṛṣadvatī/Āpayā [RV 3.23.4]. Nadīstuti list Gaṅgā → Suṣomā incl. Vitastā, Asiknī [RV 10.75.5–6]; soma-places Suṣomā, Śaryāṇāvat [RV 8.7.29].
Campaign & Migration Vectors Bharata crossing of Vipāś–Śutudrī [RV 3.33.11–12]; Dāśarājña at the Paruṣṇī [RV 7.18, 7.83]. Direction unattested; both E→W and W→E readings are published. Pūrus: Paruṣṇī [RV 7.18.13] and Sarasvatī [RV 7.96.2], both Layer 1. Brāhmaṇa memory of Sudās's Aindra Mahābhiṣeka [AB 8.21.11, as cited by the RAG]; Prācī is a direction in the AB quarter scheme [AB 8.14]. Pūru line → Kuru name: Kuruśravaṇa Trāsadasyava [RV 10.33.4, L2]; the Pūru west → east line is a hypothesis.
Political Geography Bharata–Pūru hostility attested [RV 7.8.4]; Pūru role at the Dāśarājña is a contested reading of RV 7.18.13. Kuru-Pañcāla polity. A Pūru–Bharata "merger" is a scholarly inference; the v1 support (RV 1.44.3 purupriya = "beloved of many") does not show it.
Settlement & Ritual Core River-bank sacrifice (Agni set on the three rivers, RV 3.23.4); parvata and river imagery. Fixed altars and Agnicayana (ŚB 6.1 ff. — ritual cosmogony; names no place).

L3 · AVŚ / YV Cumuri, Dhuni, Namuci [AVŚ 20.37.4–5, as cited by the RAG]. AVŚ book 20 repeats Rigvedic hymns, so this is not independent Layer-3 evidence; cite the RV source.
L4 · Brāhmaṇas Combat language reused liturgically [AB 5.8, as cited by the RAG; not checked]. No place data. The shift is semantic/ritual, not a map movement.

Net result for the map: one Dāsa marker (Śambara's mountains, schematic), one Dāsa dot on the Paruṣṇī sites (RV 7.83.1), and a roster of named figures in the Śambara dossier. Anything more specific (Kabul–Swat, a Doab shift) is interpretation, not verse.
